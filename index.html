05How to read this page · what each number means and why it matters

AThe score (0–100)

Every period starts at 100, which would mean no problems were found in audits or in patient tickets. Each problem found takes points away, and the more serious the problem, the more points it costs.

≥ 85On track: problems are rare.
75–85Watch: a few problem areas need attention.
< 75Action needed: problems are frequent or serious.

One number for Apollo 24|7’s overall clinical compliance that the board can track month by month.

BThe gauge and the points-lost bar

The gauge shows which band the score falls in. The bar runs from 0 to 100: the green part is the score kept, and each red piece is the points lost to one type of problem. Darker red means a more serious problem.

The longest red piece is the biggest gap, and so the best place to focus improvement.

CArrows and comparisons

▲ green means better (the score went up, or fewer points were lost). ▼ red means worse. A full month is compared with the month before it; a month-to-date period with the same days of last month.

When the earlier period’s audit data is incomplete, the audit-based comparisons are hidden so the board never sees a misleading change.

Tells you whether things are improving, not just where they stand.

Critical errors · 4 points each

Audits that scored 0%, the audit checklist’s critical-error outcome. Measured as a % of all scored audits; every 1% costs 4 points.

The highest patient-safety risk, so it carries the heaviest weight. Even a small rise here should be investigated.

High-severity tickets · 3 points each

Patient complaints with direct clinical impact, such as prescription issues, wrong or expired medicines, cold-chain failures, wrong lab reports or hematoma. Measured per 1,000 orders; each one costs 3 points.

A patient was directly affected. These usually need root-cause review with the doctor, pharmacy or lab team.

Medium-severity tickets · 2 points each

Complaints about service quality or delays, such as a doctor not replying within 24 hours, incomplete or damaged orders, partial reports or re-sampling. Measured per 1,000 orders; each one costs 2 points.

They hurt patient experience and trust, and repeats point to process gaps that can be fixed.

Non-critical audit misses · 1 point each

Points auditors took off on the non-critical checklist items, measured as 100 minus the average score of audits that scored above 0. Each point missed costs 1 point.

Shows the everyday quality of consultations, such as documentation and communication, beyond the critical errors.

DBusiness-line cards

Consult, Pharmacy and Diagnostics are each measured against their own orders, so a small line is judged as fairly as a big one. The score uses the average of the three. The outlined card is the line losing the most points to tickets. The small bar splits those points between High (dark) and Medium (light).

Shows which team owns the biggest share of patient escalations.

EWeekly trend and audit coverage

The line shows the score week by week against the colored bands. Hollow dots mark weeks where the audit sheets no longer hold all audits, so those weeks are not like-for-like.

Audit coverage is audits ÷ consults (target 2–3%). It isn’t part of the score; it shows how much of the work was checked.

A steady trend inside the green band, with coverage on target, is the goal.

What moves the score

Points gained for each improvement (the same arithmetic the score uses).

Critical-error audits fall by 0.25% of audits (for example from 0.76% to 0.51%)+1.0
1 fewer High-severity ticket per 1,000 orders (average of the three lines)+3.0
1 fewer Medium-severity ticket per 1,000 orders (average of the three lines)+2.0
Average score of non-zero audits rises by 1 point (for example 93.6% to 94.6%)+1.0

Where the data comes from

Everything is read live from the Google Sheets each time the page opens; “Data through” at the top shows the latest day available.

  • Audits: PDS, Non Pharma, PDS–Mounjaro and BS WLP audit sheets (critical errors and other misses).
  • Patient tickets: the ticket sheet’s Data (Consult, Diagnostics) and Medicine tabs, with High/Medium set by category.
  • Orders: the ticket sheet’s Orders tab (consult, pharma, diagnostics), used to turn tickets into rates.
  • Consults: Total_Consults Extract 1, for audit coverage.

Full method, the High/Medium category list and links to every sheet are on Page 2 · Compliance Deep-dive.

How the score works. Every period starts at 100 and loses points for each problem found, heaviest first: 4 points per 1% of audits with a critical error (zero score), 3 points per High-severity ticket per 1,000 orders, 2 points per Medium-severity ticket per 1,000 orders, and 1 point per audit point missed on non-critical parameters. Ticket rates are the average of Consult, Pharmacy and Diagnostics, each against its own orders. RAG: green ≥ 85, amber 75–85, red < 75. Full method, High/Medium list and sources on the deep-dive page.
